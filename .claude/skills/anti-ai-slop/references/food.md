# Restaurants, menus and ordering sites

Learned on a real late-night delivery app (Pidi, Aruba, Oct 2026).

## Where the true details are

- **The kitchen's own recipes** (recipe cards, prep sheets, the chef's notes): what really
  goes in, how it's cooked, how it's packed. Read them before rewriting a dish. When the
  menu and the recipe disagree (a patty count, a sauce, an ingredient), that's a finding.
- **Allergens come from the recipe, not from the menu text.** A marinade (buttermilk is
  milk), a garnish (sesame seeds), a sauce (oyster sauce is shellfish) or a dip (mustard)
  is easy to miss. Under-declaring an allergen is a safety problem, not a style problem:
  fix it at once and tell the owner.
- **Operations:** hours, last orders, delivery areas by name, the fee, how to pay, how the
  order arrives (WhatsApp, phone, app). These are the specifics customers need first.

## Tells specific to food sites

- Every dish on the same dark slate with steam: one prompt for the whole menu.
- Descriptions made of adjectives ("mouthwatering, perfectly seasoned, bursting with
  flavor") instead of ingredients.
- The same tagline shape on every brand or section ("A, B & C").
- The cover photo repeated as the first featured dish right under it.
- "Our Signature Creations", "Culinary journey", "Fiesta in every bite".
- Ratings and "best in town" with no source.

## What works

- **Dish text:** what's in it, in the order you'd see it, then the one detail that
  matters ("with a cup of consommé to dip", "cut on a bias, with mustard").
- **Taps, not typing**, for choices the kitchen can really do with stock it has (no onion,
  sauce on the side).
- **The real container:** a photo of the actual box, tray or bag is the most trusted
  picture a delivery kitchen can show.
- **Honest labels:** "Pictures are illustrations." until they're real; "Sold out today"
  when it is.
- **Prices exactly as charged**, one currency line ("Prices in Aruban florin (ƒ)").
