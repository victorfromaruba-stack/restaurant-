# Pages and screens: what looks generated, and what to do instead

Ordered by how fast a visitor notices. Keep the brand's real identity (its colours, its
type, its own marks); remove the generator defaults around it.

| Tell | Why it reads generated | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Purple-to-pink or indigo gradients, gradient text, "aurora" blobs | The default palette of every generated landing page | Flat colours from the brand. A dark scrim over a photo is the one gradient that earns its place |
| Sparkle star icons (✦, ✨, a four-point star) | It is the icon of AI products; people read it as "a bot made this" | The business's own mark, a wordmark, or nothing |
| Glass cards: blur, transparency, glowing borders and shadows | Generator default, and it muddies the text | Solid surfaces separated by a hairline or a tint |
| Pills on everything: badges, chips, info tags, buttons | The app-template look. Chips that can't be tapped pretend to be buttons | Pills only for things you tap. Facts as one plain line: "Late night from 10 PM · ƒ5 delivery" |
| Emoji as icons (🚀 🍹 🔥 ✅) | Placeholder-grade; renders differently on every phone | A word, or a simple drawn icon where it helps a tap |
| "Why choose us": three identical cards with an icon, a title and a sentence | The single most templated section on the web | Say the one thing that's true in a sentence, or show a number |
| Testimonials carousel, star rows, "Trusted by" logo strip | Usually invented, always suspected | Real quotes only, with permission. Nothing is better than fake |
| Every heading huge, condensed and in capitals; everything centred | When everything shouts, nothing does | One loud thing per screen. Left-aligned text, sentence-case headings |
| Pulsing or bouncing buttons, countdowns, "12 people viewing" | Fake urgency; people know the trick | Motion only for real state changes |
| Huge rounded corners and huge soft shadows on everything | UI-kit default | Smaller radii, borders over shadows |
| Template fonts with no reason (Inter, Poppins, Montserrat everywhere) | The first thing that makes a page look like every other | The brand's font, or a system font set with real care for size and spacing |
| A hero, a features grid, testimonials, pricing tiers, FAQ and a "Ready to get started?" band, in that order | The generated page skeleton | Only the sections the reader needs to decide, in the order they need them |
| Stock or generated people smiling at laptops | Nobody believes them | Real people with permission, or no people |

## Phones first

Most visitors are on a phone, often in a hurry. Check the first screen at 390px wide (and
320px for small phones): what's there is the whole page for most people. It should answer
"what is this, is it open or available, what does it cost, how do I get it" without a scroll.

## What real looks like

- Real details on the first screen: the hours, the place, the price, the way to order or
  book.
- A layout with one big thing and smaller things, not identical boxes.
- Pictures that show the actual product, place or people, used once each.
- Honest empty states: "No reviews yet" is better than invented ones (or simply no section).
- Small imperfections are fine. Perfect symmetry everywhere is a tell.
